## A city model is not yet a digital twin

Three-dimensional visualization can help people understand buildings, infrastructure and urban form. But visual fidelity alone does not create a useful decision system.

A practical urban digital twin should connect **geospatial context, trusted operational data, analytical models and scenario workflows**.

## Building the foundation

- **Terrain and elevation:** physical surfaces and constraints.
- **Buildings and infrastructure:** built environments, from open 3D data to BIM.
- **Land use and planning:** rules, capacity and development scenarios.
- **Environmental and operational signals:** observations that change over time.

The challenge is not to put every dataset into one viewer. It is to establish consistent identifiers, update cycles, quality expectations and useful questions.

## Designing for decisions

Start with a decision, then work backward to the data, models and interfaces needed to support it. This keeps the digital twin focused on outcomes rather than visual complexity.
